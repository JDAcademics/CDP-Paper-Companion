Z - Community Needs Analysis as Assessment Tool

💡 Core Idea

Community Needs Analysis (CNA) is a systematic, participatory process that captures local knowledge and lived experiences to identify infrastructure deficiencies and prioritize improvement needs — complementing technical engineering assessments and providing justification for design decisions.

📖 Explanation

Definition

A systematic process used to identify and assess transportation-related concerns, roadway deficiencies, and infrastructure needs of residents within a study area.

Methods Used in This Study

  • Stakeholder consultations
  • Surveys (371 respondents via Cochran's formula, 95% confidence)
  • Interviews
  • Field observations
  • Hazard identification activities
  • Engineering assessments

Why CNA Matters

  • Captures concerns not visible in official records
  • Provides community perspective on safety and accessibility
  • Identifies priorities based on user experience
  • Supports development of responsive, practical solutions
  • Complements technical assessments with local knowledge
  • Provides justification for design scope and interventions

Key Finding from This Study

Due to limited formal accident records, roadway safety concerns were assessed through community surveys, interviews, consultations, field observations, hazard identification, and engineering assessments.

Community-Identified Needs (9 items)

  1. Adequate drainage integration
  2. Appropriate roadway slope and cross slope
  3. Adequate roadway lighting
  4. Regulatory and warning road signs
  5. Roadway curve and superelevation improvements
  6. Roadside crash barriers and protective features
  7. Adequate roadway width
  8. Pavement repair and rehabilitation
  9. Comprehensive roadway improvement

🎯 Key Principle

Community-based approaches, field observations, hazard identification, and engineering assessments are valuable tools for understanding existing roadway conditions and determining infrastructure priorities.

LN - Chapter 2: Review of Related Literature (Updated)

📚 Overview

The literature review establishes theoretical, technical, and conceptual foundations by examining local and international studies, engineering standards, government guidelines, and implemented projects (prior art) related to:

  • Farm-to-Market Roads
  • Roadway safety
  • Pavement deterioration, maintenance, and structural design
  • Traffic characteristics
  • Roadway geometry
  • Roadway safety devices (signs, markings, lighting, barriers)
  • Drainage systems
  • Transportation accessibility
  • Comprehensive roadway improvement and infrastructure sustainability

📖 Major Themes (Expanded)

2.2.1 Farm-to-Market Roads and Rural Transportation Development

  • FMRs connect agricultural production areas with communities, markets, essential services
  • Vila (2019): Philippine study — FMR pavement condition significantly affected agricultural yield, net sales, post-harvest losses, travel time, travel speed in Camiling, Tarlac
  • Fruelda et al. (2025): GIS-based assessment of road infrastructure and traffic dynamics in Odiongan, Romblon; identified 12 barangays with poor road conditions; traffic dominated by motorcycles/tricycles
  • International evidence: India (rural connectivity → household economic activities, agricultural technology adoption); Ethiopia (road access → agricultural commercialization)
  • DA-BAFE (2024), Porter (2014), Starkey & Hine (2014), World Bank (2022), ADB (2021): Rural infrastructure supports mobility, livelihoods, access
  • Key point: FMR should be evaluated by physical condition, transportation function, traffic characteristics, and ability to provide reliable access — not just existence of road

→ Z - Farm-to-Market Roads as Rural Lifelines

2.2.2 Roadway Safety and Road Safety Features

  • Dhakal & Al-Kaisy (2024): Approach for identifying safety-improvement locations on rural highways without extensive crash-history data using traffic volume, geometric characteristics, roadside features
  • Khan et al. (2023): Geometric design consistency and run-off-road crashes on two-lane rural highways
  • Shallam et al. (2022): Geometric consistency considering different vehicle types (passenger cars and trucks)
  • Goyani et al. (2024): Geometric design consistency using crash-severity information
  • Motalib et al. (2024): Philippine study — Sagonsongan Diversion Road, Marawi City; examined horizontal/vertical alignment, superelevation; identified inconsistencies with safety requirements; proposed corrective measures
  • Kučina et al. (2023): Signs and pavement markings on horizontal curves in rural roads
  • WHO (2023), Mcllroy et al. (2022), Montella (2005), PIARC (2019), AASHTO (2018): Systems-based approach; safety assessment through actual roadway conditions

→ Z - Roadway Safety as a Systems Problem

2.2.3 Pavement Deterioration and Roadway Maintenance

  • Sunitha & Babu (2012): Pavement-deterioration models for low-volume rural roads; considered pavement distress, shoulder condition, drainage features, cross-drainage structures, cross slope
  • Han et al. (2023): Long-term maintenance planning for rural roads under limited budgets (280-km road network, Haimen City, China); prioritize sections by condition and expected performance
  • Vila (2019): FMR pavement condition affects farmers' travel time, travel speed, yield, net sales, post-harvest losses
  • Fruelda et al. (2025): Roadway-condition assessment for identifying rural road sections requiring maintenance/rehabilitation
  • Cambodia study: Rural-road pavement alternatives under heavy truck traffic and rainfall; accelerated deterioration when traffic/environmental exposure exceeds original design
  • Basnet et al. (2023), Ji et al. (2023), AASHTO (2018): Traffic loading, environmental exposure, materials, drainage as factors

→ Z - Pavement Deterioration and Proactive Maintenance

2.2.4 Traffic Characteristics and Traffic Data for Roadway Design (NEW)

  • Fruelda et al. (2025): Classified traffic information useful for evaluating rural roadway performance; differences between peak/off-peak; motorcycles/tricycles large proportion
  • Zhao et al. (2022): Relationship between traffic loading and pavement performance using truck and axle-load information
  • Rys & Burnos (2022): Axle-load spectra for pavement design; total traffic volume alone may not adequately represent pavement demand
  • Ordaz et al. (2024): Structural deterioration of low-volume pavement under increased traffic loading; reassess when traffic conditions change
  • Popoola et al. (2018): Traffic volume, heavy-goods-vehicle proportion, roadway geometry, shoulder conditions, traffic signs associated with roadway safety
  • AASHTO (2018), FHWA: Traffic demand and vehicle characteristics as important inputs; distinguish volume, classification, and weight information

→ Z - Traffic Characteristics Drive Design Decisions

2.2.5 Pavement Structural Design and Rehabilitation Considerations (NEW)

  • Philippine study on rehabilitation of concrete roads: Field trials at 5 sites (~9.5 km); developed rehabilitation guidance based on observed distress
  • Muslimah, Subagio, & Frazila (2023): Structural condition of rigid pavement in Bandung, Indonesia using AASHTO 1993 and MDP 2017; different procedures produce different rehabilitation requirements
  • Ordaz et al. (2024): Structural deterioration under increased traffic loading
  • Zhao et al. (2022), Rys & Burnos (2022): Axle-load characteristics influence pavement deterioration
  • Nondestructive pavement evaluation: Structural-response measurements for determining stiffness and capacity
  • DPWH standards: Minimum pavement requirements; higher thicknesses should be verified through pavement-design analysis
  • Key point: Existing 150-mm PCCP should not automatically be assumed adequate under current conditions; proposed 200-mm PCCP must be supported by structural calculations

→ Z - Pavement Structural Design vs Condition Assessment

2.2.6 Roadway Geometry and Transportation Safety

  • Motalib et al. (2024): Philippine study — Sagonsongan Diversion Road, Marawi City; field surveying and AutoCAD Civil 3D analysis
  • Khan et al. (2023), Shallam et al. (2022), Goyani et al. (2024): Geometric consistency and crash risk on rural two-lane highways
  • Popoola et al. (2018): Curve radius, lane width, shoulder characteristics, access roads, traffic volume, heavy-goods-vehicle proportion, traffic signs associated with safety
  • Dhakal & Al-Kaisy (2024): Traffic volume, geometric features, roadside characteristics for identifying safety-improvement locations
  • Chen et al. (2022), Montella (2005), PIARC (2019), AASHTO (2018): Geometric characteristics important components of roadway safety
  • Key point: Project does not presume complete realignment; localized interventions may include geometric correction, cross slope/superelevation improvement, sight-distance measures, curve warning signs, chevron markers, pavement markings, roadside protection

→ Z - Roadway Geometry Influences Safety and Drainage

2.2.7 Roadway Lighting, Traffic Signs, and Roadside Safety Devices

  • Kučina et al. (2023): Curves important locations for safety treatments
  • Elvik (2009): Road-lighting effects using accident data; differences in injury-accident risk between lit and unlit roads
  • Active road studs research: Reflective/active delineation devices improve driver confidence and guidance at night
  • Bermudo & Rabacal: Philippine study — traffic signs and road-user knowledge/practices in Camaligan, Camarines Sur
  • Roadside safety research: Appropriate roadside protection reduces severity of run-off-road events when warranted
  • Robielos & Lin (2022), Bullough et al. (2013), Plainis et al. (2020), Calderón Ramírez et al. (2023): Supporting research
  • DPWH standards: Criteria for road signs, pavement markings, delineation, streetlighting, roadside barriers
  • Key point: Site-specific and warranted safety measures; continuous lighting or uniform installation not automatically assumed

→ Z - Roadway Safety Devices Save Lives

2.2.8 Drainage Systems and Stormwater Management

  • Iskandar et al.: Relationship between drainage condition and pavement performance using PCI analysis
  • Echatu (2023): Rural-road drainage systems in Katakwi District, Uganda; inadequate design, poor maintenance, flooding, topography, construction-related factors
  • Essien et al. (2024): Hydrologic performance of highway filter drains; evaluate runoff and drainage capacity
  • Jo & Kim (2024): Road-surface runoff under different rainfall and slope conditions; roadway slope and rainfall influence runoff behavior
  • Roadside gutter systems research: Sediment accumulation and maintenance conditions can reduce drainage capacity
  • Basnet et al. (2023), Ji et al. (2023), Fletcher et al. (2021), PIARC (2019), AASHTO (2018), DPWH, DA-BAFE (2024): Supporting literature
  • Key point: Drainage treated as integrated roadway component; potential interventions include concrete gutters, curb-and-gutter, localized structures, improved runoff paths

→ Z - Drainage Systems Protect Infrastructure and Users

2.2.9 Transportation Accessibility and Community Mobility

  • Vila (2019): FMR pavement condition affects travel time, travel speed, agricultural yield, net sales, post-harvest losses
  • Kamaludin & Qibthiyyah (2022): Village-road quality and accessibility in Indonesia; improvements associated with rural economic transformation
  • Rong & Ouyang (2024): Rural road accessibility and healthcare-seeking behavior in China; improvements increased treatment at higher-level hospitals
  • Mckonnen & Alamirew (2023): Rural road access and agricultural commercialization in Ethiopia
  • Geurs & van Wee (2004), Lucas (2012), Porter (2014), Starkey & Hine (2014), World Bank (2022), ADB (2021): Theoretical basis
  • Key point: Accessibility treated as outcome of adequately functioning roadway, not separate facility

→ Z - Transportation Accessibility Enables Community Development

2.2.10 Comprehensive Roadway Improvement and Infrastructure Sustainability

  • Fruelda et al. (2025): Combined roadway-condition assessment and traffic analysis using GIS in Odiongan, Romblon
  • Li et al. (2024): Sustainability constraints affecting rural-road infrastructure in China; economic, management, policy, public-participation, terrain-related constraints
  • Han et al. (2023): Long-term maintenance-planning method under limited budgets
  • Hasan et al. (2022): Rural-road planning and prioritization methodology using physical, economic, social criteria
  • Argoty Burbano (2023): Technical-economic alternatives for rural-road rehabilitation
  • Ikuabe et al. (2021): Sustainable rural-road infrastructure and rural development
  • Kamaludin & Qibthiyyah (2022): Village-road quality improvements associated with rural economic transformation
  • AASHTO (2018), PIARC (2019), WHO (2023), DPWH standards: Integrated perspective
  • Key point: Interventions prioritized according to identified deficiencies, technical requirements, site conditions, available resources — not uniform treatments

🧩 Conceptual Framework

Input-Process-Output (IPO) Framework (Updated)

Component Elements
INPUT CNA findings, existing roadway conditions, field observations and site assessment, survey responses, stakeholder consultations, traffic and vehicle classification data, historical project and geotechnical data, applicable engineering standards and related studies
PROCESS Data gathering and validation, site visits and field inspection, traffic and heavy-vehicle analysis, pavement condition and structural evaluation, roadway geometry and safety assessment, drainage and roadside condition assessment, identification and evaluation of improvement alternatives, engineering design and technical evaluation, quantity take-off and preliminary cost estimation
OUTPUT Proposed Comprehensive Roadway Improvement Design: pavement improvement and structural design, integrated drainage improvements, roadway geometry and safety improvements, traffic-control/lighting/roadside safety devices, improved accessibility and roadway serviceability, quantity take-off and preliminary cost estimate, construction and maintenance considerations

→ Z - Input-Process-Output Framework

🔧 Operational Framework (NEW)

The Operational Framework presents systematic sequence:

  1. Data Gathering: CNA, site/field assessment, traffic survey, existing roadway/drainage data, historical project records, codes and standards review
  2. Roadway Assessment: Pavement condition, traffic analysis, geometry, drainage, road safety, identification of critical locations
  3. Preliminary Roadway Design: Pavement design (200-mm PCCP proposed), roadway/geometric improvements, integrated drainage, road safety/traffic-control, roadside/lighting
  4. Structural Design: Design criteria, loads, one-way slab design, slab thickness, reinforcement, serviceability, pavement structural design (traffic/vehicle loading, PCCP thickness verification)
  5. Structural Analysis/Modeling: One-way slab analysis, load determination, load combinations, structural analysis, moment/shear, reinforcement requirements, pavement structural evaluation
  6. Design Validation: Verify one-way slab (strength, serviceability, reinforcement), verify pavement design (structural requirements, 200-mm PCCP adequacy), check compatibility, revise/finalize
  7. Final Drawings, Estimation & Scheduling: Integrated 2D plans in AutoCAD (roadway plan/profile, typical cross-sections, drainage, road safety/traffic-control, other details), 3D roadway model, quantity take-off, cost estimate, BOQ, project scheduling and Gantt chart
  8. Project Output: Comprehensive Roadway Improvement Design (Sta. 0+000 to Sta. 0+850)

🔑 Key Conclusions from Literature

  1. FMRs critical for agricultural productivity, economic development, community connectivity
  2. Roadway performance influenced by interrelated components: pavement, traffic, geometry, drainage, safety devices, lighting, accessibility
  3. Deficiencies negatively affect safety, efficiency, accessibility, serviceability
  4. Preventive maintenance and effective drainage preserve roadway integrity
  5. Pavement structural design differs from condition assessment; must consider traffic loading, existing structure, subgrade, drainage
  6. Traffic characterization (classified data, heavy vehicles) essential for pavement and geometric design
  7. Community-based approaches and engineering assessments valuable for identifying concerns
  8. Integrated, site-specific, standards-based approach needed — not uniform treatments
  9. Sustainability depends on planning, management, resources, and site conditions

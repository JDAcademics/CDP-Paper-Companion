LN - Chapter 1: Introduction and Project Overview (Updated)

📌 Project Title

Better Roads for the Community: Proposed Roadway Improvement Design for the Phase 1 Farm-to-Market Road in Barangay San Isidro, Catanauan, Quezon

🎯 Research Question

"How can the existing farm-to-market road in Barangay San Isidro be improved to ensure safer, more efficient, and sustainable transportation for the community?"

📍 Study Context

  • Location: Barangay San Isidro, Catanauan, Quezon
  • Population: 4,316 residents | 1,084 households | 1,173 families
  • Primary Livelihood: Agriculture (farming and copra production)
  • Road Length: 12.36 km (Phase 1 FMR); detailed design on 850 m (Sta. 0+000 to Sta. 0+850)
  • CNA Respondents: 371 (Cochran's formula, 95% confidence level)
  • Implementing Project: PRDP Sub-Project No. PRDP-ID-RO4A-041-CAT-001-2017
  • Existing Pavement: 150-mm PCCP
  • Proposed Pavement: 200-mm PCCP (to be verified by structural analysis)

🌍 Geographical Location (NEW)

  • Figure 1.1 shows Google Earth map of the Phase 1 FMR
  • Coordinates identify exact location and extent
  • 12.36 km total; detailed design area is Sta. 0+000 to Sta. 0+850

⚠️ Hazard Assessment (NEW)

Seismic Hazards (HazardHunterPH)

Hazard Finding Recommendation
Ground Rupture Safe; nearest active fault (Philippine Fault: Guinayangan Segment) ~11.5 km away; outside 5-m buffer zone Site investigation and alignment verification recommended
Ground Shaking Potential Intensity VIII May cause pavement cracking, ground deformation; design should account for seismic resilience
Liquefaction Safe Site-specific geotechnical investigation recommended
Earthquake-Induced Landslide Data being updated Geotechnical investigation and slope stability assessment recommended
Tsunami Safe Maintain drainage and road maintenance

Volcanic Hazards

Hazard Finding Recommendation
Lahar Safe; nearest volcano (Mount Banahaw) ~90.6 km away Drainage and maintenance recommended
Pyroclastic Flow Safe Monitor volcanic activity; maintain road accessibility

Hydro-Meteorological Hazards

Hazard Finding Recommendation
Flood High susceptibility (1–2 m flood height; >3 days duration) Hydrological and geotechnical investigation; adequate drainage; road elevation
Rainfall-Induced Landslide Cannot be disregarded Geotechnical investigation; slope protection; drainage; erosion control
Severe Wind 117.1–220 kph (20-yr); 220.1–270 kph (500-yr) May affect road safety due to fallen trees/debris
Storm Surge Data being updated Monitor PAGASA advisories; site-specific investigation

Key Takeaway

The site is generally safe from most seismic and volcanic hazards but has high flood susceptibility and severe wind exposure. Design must integrate drainage, slope protection, and climate-resilient measures.

👥 Community and Their Needs (NEW)

Community Profile

  • Rural agricultural community
  • Livelihood: farming, copra production, trading of agricultural products
  • San Isidro–Sta. Maria Dao FMR (Phase 1) extends ~12.36 km
  • Provides access to schools, markets, healthcare, community services

Community-Identified Roadway Needs (9 items)

  1. Adequate drainage integration
  2. Appropriate roadway slope and cross slope
  3. Adequate roadway lighting
  4. Regulatory and warning road signs
  5. Roadway curve and superelevation improvements
  6. Roadside crash barriers and protective features
  7. Adequate roadway width
  8. Pavement repair and rehabilitation
  9. Comprehensive roadway improvement (integrated design)

🧩 Complex Engineering Problem (NEW)

ABET Characteristics

The project demonstrates characteristics of a complex engineering problem:

  • Involves multiple interrelated technical considerations
  • Several component problems
  • Potentially conflicting safety, accessibility, constructability, maintenance, and economic requirements
  • No single obvious solution
  • Requires integration of different civil engineering considerations, engineering analysis, and design decision-making

Key Challenges

  • Pavement deterioration
  • Roadway safety deficiencies
  • Limited signage and lighting
  • Roadway geometric concerns
  • Roadside hazards
  • Accessibility
  • Drainage-related conditions
  • Varying conditions along 850-m segment
  • Traffic characteristics
  • Applicable engineering standards
  • Constructability, maintenance, economic feasibility

🎯 Objectives

General Objective

Develop a safe, accessible, technically appropriate, and cost-efficient Comprehensive Roadway Improvement Design for the selected 850-m segment from Sta. 0+000 to Sta. 0+850 of the Phase 1 FMR, in accordance with applicable Philippine engineering codes, standards, and guidelines.

Specific Objectives

  1. Assess existing condition of selected segment and identify deficiencies (pavement, safety, geometric, accessibility, traffic control, lighting, roadside, drainage)
  2. Develop and evaluate appropriate roadway improvement alternatives
  3. Determine appropriate improvement solution based on technical, safety, accessibility, constructability, maintenance, and economic considerations
  4. Prepare proposed Comprehensive Roadway Improvement Design (engineering plans, pavement design, quantity take-off, preliminary cost estimate, construction and implementation considerations)

👥 Significance / Beneficiaries

  • LGUs — Technical reference for planning, evaluating, prioritizing, implementing roadway improvement projects
  • Barangay Officials — Engineering-based information for infrastructure planning, project proposals, maintenance
  • Community Residents & Road Users — Safer, more efficient travel conditions
  • Engineers & Infrastructure Planners — Reference for similar rural settings
  • Future Researchers — Basis for further studies

📏 Scope & Limitations (Updated)

Included

  • Assessment and design for 850-m segment (Sta. 0+000 to Sta. 0+850)
  • 12.36-km FMR considered as broader study context
  • Pavement condition and structural requirements
  • Roadway geometry and critical locations
  • Available traffic information and vehicle characteristics
  • Traffic-control devices and requirements
  • Roadway lighting
  • Roadside safety and protection
  • Accessibility
  • Drainage-related conditions
  • Development and evaluation of improvement alternatives
  • Pavement design, engineering plans, quantity take-off, cost estimate, construction considerations

Excluded / Delimited

  • Complete geometric redesign or realignment
  • New geotechnical investigation, field soil testing, independent soil characterization
  • Comprehensive hydrologic or hydraulic analysis
  • Current AADT or traffic growth rate (unless sufficient data)
  • Political/administrative decisions, funding allocation, procurement, maintenance-fund management
  • Actual construction, contractor selection, procurement, post-construction monitoring
  • Remaining portions of 12.36-km roadway (beyond broader context)

Key Limitation

Due to limited formal accident records, roadway safety concerns were assessed through community surveys, interviews, field observations, hazard identification, and engineering assessments rather than official accident statistics.
